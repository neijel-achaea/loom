# Code of conduct

Loom is made by players, for players. Everyone who takes part (opening an issue, commenting, reviewing, sending a pull request) should be able to do so without being made to feel unwelcome.

## Where it applies

This code covers Loom's spaces on GitHub: issues, pull requests, discussions, reviews and commits in this repository. How players treat each other in Achaea is the game's business, under the game's own rules.

## What we expect

- **Be kind and assume good intent.** Most people here are fellow players giving up their own time.
- **Be patient with newcomers.** Not knowing how Git, Nexus scripting or a class works yet is fine; everyone started there.
- **Criticise ideas, not people.** Say what's wrong with a change and why, not what's wrong with the person who made it.
- **Accept a decision gracefully**, even when it isn't the one you wanted. Loom has a clear scope (see [Safety and fair play](docs/safety.md)), and some requests won't fit it.
- **Keep in-game rivalries in game.** City, house or order conflicts don't belong here, and neither do in-character insults.

## What isn't acceptable

- Harassment, insults, slurs, or personal attacks, in public or in private messages.
- Sexual language or imagery, or unwelcome attention of any kind.
- Discrimination, or jokes at the expense of anyone's identity.
- Sharing someone's private information (their real name, contact details, other characters) without their permission.
- Trolling, deliberate disruption, or repeatedly pushing a request that has been declined.
- Pressing for features that would break the game's rules on automation, or sharing changes that turn Loom's packages into bots.

## Reporting

If something here makes you uncomfortable, or you see someone being treated badly:

- **For a private report**, send an in-game message to the maintainer, **Neijel**, in Achaea: `MSG Neijel <your message>`. Messages wait if he's offline. Say it's about Loom's code of conduct, and include links to what happened if you can. Reports are read only by the maintainer and kept confidential.
- **For abusive content** (harassment, spam), you can also use GitHub's own *Report content* option, from the "…" menu of any issue, comment or pull request. That goes to GitHub's staff.

Please don't report conduct problems in a public issue.

## What happens next

The maintainer will look at every report, reply to the person who made it, and decide what to do. Depending on what happened, that may be a private word, a public reminder of this code, removing comments, or blocking someone from the repository, temporarily or for good. Whoever is involved will be told what was decided and why, unless doing so would put someone at risk.
